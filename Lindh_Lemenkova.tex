\abtitle{P-wave velocity and compressive strength of stabilised soil}
\title{P-wave velocity and compressive strength of soil stabilised by various binder ratios for SCA Biorefinery Östrand AB, Timrå}

\abauthor{Author-1, Author-2}
\author{Author-1$^{1}$$^{2}$\email{email@blind.review} \\ Author-2$^{3}$\email{email@blind.review}}

\Abstract{\hl{Soil stabilisation and solidification were undertaken in this study} in order to assess \hl{the} compression characteristics \hl{by changed binder ratios. The work aimed to replace the contaminated masses collected in the seabed as dredged sediments that cannot be dumped into the sea for environmental reasons and safety. The use of binder (Cement (Basement CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1) and Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag) aimed to increase the compressive strength of the soil masses}. 
Experiments were performed on behalf of the SCA Biorefinery Östrand AB using dredged material from the pulp mill taken from Sk\"{o}nviken. \hl{Various quantity of binder (150, 120 and 100 kg) was used. The properties of soil were evaluated after 28, 42, 43, 70, 71 and 85 days of curing using applied geophysical methods of measuring the travel time of primary wave propagation. The P-wave velocities were determined along with the compression characteristics of stabilised soils.} The results demonstrated variation of P-waves velocity depending on stabilizing agent and curing time in various ratios: Low water / High binder ($L_WH_B$), High water / Low binder ($H_WL_B$) and percentage of agents (CEM II / A-V/GGBFS) as 30\%/70\%, 50\%/50\% and 70\%/30\%. The compression characteristics of soils were assessed using Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS). The P-wave velocities were higher for samples stabilized with $L_WH_B$ compared to those stabilized with ratio $H_WL_B$. The primary wave propagation increased over curing time for all stabilized mixes along with the increased UCS, which proves \hl{a tight} correlation with the increased strength of soil solidified by the agents. Increased water ratio gives a lower strength while maintaining the amount of binder and vice versa.}

\Keywords{Unconfined Compressive Strength; P-wave velocity; stabilized soil; cement; slag}

\section{Introduction}

Accurate evaluation of the soil strength plays a major role in the design of a safe construction on a foundation ground. The SCA Biorefinery Östrand AB plans to build a new biorefinery for the production of fuel in form of petrol and diesel from the biological raw materials. This construction is planned to extend the existing infrastructure of the pulp mill in Östrand's industrial area located in Timrå municipality, northern Sweden. The soils in the area prepared for the planned biorefinery need to be stabilized and solidified before the construction works. In addition, extra area needs to be cleaned in a coastal area of the Gulf of Bothnia, Baltic Sea, in order to gain more space for the planned new biorefinery, Fig. \ref{fig:01}. 

Assessment of compressive strength of soils is important before the construction works, for evaluating ground stability aimed to ensure the safety and sustainability of structures \cite{Czarnecki}. Choosing the most optimal combination of binder (cement, slag or other materials) is the most important parameter of the soil stabilization and solidification (S/S), as reported in previous studies \cite{Lindh2001,Xizhong,LindhWinter}. At the same time, it is important in geotechnical engineering to select the best proportion of binders to ensure the strength of soils. The impact of various combination of binders and ratio of water for stabilising soils can be assessed by measuring P-waves of soil samples comparatively assessed after a curing period. However, when stabilising soil by various ratio of binders, it does not guarantee the best combination of binders based on the theoretical assessment. Instead, cement-slag-water ratio for stabilising soil samples should be evaluated practically, as a series of the \emph{in-situ} tests, in order to ensure the safety of soils before the planned construction works.

Various practical methods such as experimental and empirical \emph{in-situ} testing of soil samples, and computer-based modelling have been employed to analyse the compressive strength of soils. These include estimating the soil porosity \cite{Lemenkov2021a,Uyanik} and density \cite{Consoli,Lemenkov2021b}, comparison of binder combinations \cite{Bernal}, adding alkali additives as cement replacement \cite{Eyo}, economic assessment of S/S soil including costs and environmental impact \cite{Rocha}. For instance, previous studies \cite{Emeryetal} experimentally examined the suction behavior of soil stabilized with cement and lime through the development of soil-water characteristic curves, to assess bearing capacity of foundations. Microstructure can be considered to estimate the strength development in cement-stabilized silty clay, to prove that cement S/S improves the soil structure by increasing cementation bonding and reducing the pore space \cite{Horpibulsuk}. Another example \cite{Lindh2021a} assessed binder components (cement, cement kiln dust and slag) for optimal S/S of sediments from the port of Gothenburg. 

Stabilisation of soil by the cementitious binder increases its strength, because large pores in soil material become filled with binder \cite{Lindh202102}. As a result, total pore volume in a soil mass decreases, which makes it suitable for the infrastructure construction: roads, buildings or industrial spaces. Experimental changing of binders produces favourable improvements of soil properties through a combination of various binders (lime, cement, and lime-cement), which increases strength and resistance of soils. For example, \cite{Mahmood} proved that adding lime to the soil mass before stabilising it with cement improves its physical and mechanical properties. Another work \cite{Suetal2009} introduced a S/S technology for treating soil contaminated with phenol choosing Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) and carbon in various proportions of binder. These tests demonstrated that soil could be effectively treated by the cement/activated-carbon S/S approach. Based on the evaluation of the uniaxial compressive strength, \cite{Diamantis} investigated the relationships among the physical, dynamic and mechanical properties of the selected rocks. 

Soil evaluation prior to engineering construction works  is commonly used in civil engineering. It is generally based on the assessment of the empirical relations between soil parameters and geotechnical applications \cite{BrunarskiDohojda,CzarneckiGemert,Kallenetal2016}. It forms the fundamental basis for evaluation of the foundation capacity for estimating its suitability for building structures. The soil may be processed from the \hl{\emph{in-situ}} collected samples using laboratory standard tests \hl{to measure discontinuities in properties of clayey sediments in the coastal areas}. \hl{Recent approaches tend to apply} the non-destructive and approaches \hl{in} soil testing. \hl{Ultrasonic testing by P-wave velocities, is a rapidly growing application in evaluation of the stabilized soils}. As an alternative to the traditional methods, the use of geophysical approaches in civil engineering, such as measuring P-waves velocities, becomes a solution to this challenge, \hl{as} remote sensing methods present more robust and reliable methods in civil engineering. As a response to these needs, many studies \cite{Frattaetal2005,Zhaoetal2022,Zhangetal2021,Guetal2021,Weietal2022}, have applied geophysical methods in geotechnical measurements aimed to improve the evaluation of the soil properties by measuring P-waves propagation velocity in samples.

Based on the reviewed literature, the method of estimating wave velocity for assessing soil mechanical property can be advantageously used in both the field and laboratory under real and controlled conditions \cite{Hussien}. For instance, measuring shear wave velocity is a robust and fast means for the determination of small-strain shear modulus in soil and rock specimens \cite{Leong}. The method of wave velocity estimation computes the speed and amplitude in sound waves with which they pass through soil based on detecting signals. In this way, it provides an estimation of the soil conductivity and petrophysical properties (bulk density, porosity, clay content, strength), because the velocity of seismic waves depends on the material properties \cite{ElSayed}. For instance, seismic velocity and density are inversely related: higher density reduces the speed of seismic waves. However, since stiffness increases at faster rate compared to density, seismic velocity is faster in denser soil \cite{Shietal2010}. Besides, velocity strongly depends on porosity of the material and significantly increases even with a small decrease in porosity \cite{Foti}. 

As far as the clayey soil is concerned, \cite{Cheshomi} and \cite{Wuetal2021} presented experimental tests based on measurements of the shear wave velocity for estimation of stiffness and strength of the soft and silty soil. Other researchers further developed investigations on relationship between soil strength and wave velocities \cite{Liuetal2020}. They estimated the void ratio in soil and effective mean normal stress, for analysis of its large-strain response. Few studies have addressed the questions of cohesive behavior for the strength analysis \cite{Gecys}. As for other materials, Grabiec \cite{Grabiec2019} presented studies on compressive strength of composites by estimating the influence of biomodification of recycled concrete aggregate. Another study \cite{Manikandan} developed an optimization method for mechanical and tribological properties of the selected composites through increased compressive strength and hardness, to better control and improve their fabrication. 

In this paper, the S/S of soils were undertaken as a part of the reinforcement work in the coastal area around the region of SCA Biorefinery Östrand AB. The aim of this work was to replace the contaminated masses collected in the seabed as dredged sediments that cannot be dumped into the sea for the environmental reasons and safety. Besides that, the use of binder aimed to increase the compressive strength of the soil masses, to clean sediments, bind the impurities and thereby to prevent leaching of heavy metals from the raw soil. By stabilising the dredged masses on site, the contaminated masses collected from the seabed could also be stabilized in the laboratory. The properties of soil were evaluated using applied geophysical methods of measuring velocities of P-wave that pass through the samples.

\section{Methods}
The purpose of this study is to investigate the properties of soil after stabilization / solidification (S/S), to define the best combination of binders affecting its properties. 

\subsection{Materials}
Tests were conducted on a sediment soil samples stabilised by a combination of cement (Basement CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1) and Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBFS) selected for the tests as binders. The experiment is based on measuring UCS and P-wave velocity of soil samples to evaluate the properties of soils prepared for construction of the new area of Biorefinery Östrand AB in geotechnical laboratory of Swedish Geotechnical Institute (SGI). The experiments were performed on a sediment samples taken from Sk\"{o}nviken. The experiment has been performed in a two-step workflow: Phase 1 and Phase 2, where three levels of binder quantity in kg per $m^3$ of the dredged material. In Phase 1 two levels of binder quantity per $m^3$ of the dredged material were tested: 150 kg and 120 kg. Respectively, 100 kg were tested in Phase 2. Quality requirements included following conditions: compressive strength of at least 140 kPa (ca $\geq$ 70 kPa) after 90 days; replacements in the stabilized material \textless 5 cm two years after the S/S. The quality objective regarding permeability constitutes both a geotechnical and environmental quality requirements. 

Cement (Basement CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1) and slag (GGBFS), trade name Bremen slag SS EN 15167-1) were selected as the stabilising agents for soil samples. The choice of the selected binders is explained by the quality and availability of binders. Both these binders are available on the commercial market and have been used successfully in previous projects of Geotechnical Swedish Institute (SGI), including the Arendal in the port of Gothenburg \cite{Lindh2021a}. The GGBS is one of the by-products of iron and steel, advantageous as binder due to its economic for large quantities of soil \cite{Yuksel}. Its usage is beneficial to environment due to the reduced use of OPC.

\subsection{Techniques}
The methodology of this study is based on non-destructive seismic testing of soil samples that provide a fast, robust and simple alternative technique to analysing physical and mechanical properties fo the stabilized soils. The tests included the determination of compression strength of the soil samples and P-wave velocity of the specimens in the laboratory of SGI. The workflow consisted of the evaluation of the compression strength of soil and velocity of ultrasonic P-waves for samples stabilized with cement and slag mixtures in different proportions: Low Water / High Binder ($L_WH_B$), High Water / Low Binder ($H_WL_B$), Low Water / Extra Low Binder ($L_WEL_B$). The ratio of binder varied as cement /slag as 50\%/50\% and cement/slag proportion as 30\%/70\% and 70\%/30\%. The treatment of soil samples has been performed using existing workflow developed by the Swedish Institute for Standards \cite{SIS2018b}. The tests were performed on the day of sample processing and subsequent to days 28, 43 and 70 of curing for the case of $H_WL_B$ mixture; days 28, 42 and 71 for the case of $L_WH_B$; 28 and 85 for the case of $L_WEL_B$, respectively.

The samples were cured in a laboratory of the SGI from the dredged material that was originally sampled by SCA and sent to the laboratory of SGI. The dredged material was stored in climate rooms at a constant moisture and temperature (ca. 7$^{\circ}$C). The aggregate sample B6b was divided into the two barrels with tight-fitting lids. After that one of the barrels was mixed with more water to simulate a higher water ratio of the dredged material. Before the process of specimen production it was homogenized and thinned by means of the mechanical stirring for five minutes, after which the required amount of dredged material was measured for further S/S processing.

The P-wave velocity has been measured on three cases for all collected samples in connection with tests on the uniaxial compressive strength  for relevant soil samples. The workflow applied the existing methodology of seismic velocity testing in geotechnical works, originally elaborated by the Swedish Institute for Standards \cite{SIS2018a,SIS2019}. The experiments with soil samples with $L_WH_B$ proportions of binder were performed on soil samples following the compression on the 28, 42 and 71 days after mixing soil with binder (Fig. \ref{fig:02}). 

Likewise, the experiments with soil samples with $H_WL_B$ proportions of binder were performed on samples on days 28, 43 and 70 after mixing sediments with binder (see Fig. \ref{fig:03}). The P-wave velocity was measured on the three days of curing, respectively for the both cases of ratio ($H_WL_B$ and $L_WH_B$). The measurements were recorded, statistically processed as graphs and visualised in the plots, presented in the Results section. 

The compression characteristics of the soils were determined using Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS) testing. The comparative analysis of the compressive strength for samples with different mixture ($L_WH_B$ versus $H_WL_B$) and proportions of cement/slag as 70/30, 50/50 and 30/70 were recorded, statistically processed as a graph and visualised on the respective plot with regression lines for the two cases: ratio $L_WH_B$ (green-colored line) and ratio $H_WL_B$ (magenta-colored line), see Fig. \ref{fig:04}. The compressive strength has been measured on day \nth{90} of storage at a temperature of 20$^{\circ}$C as a function of the P-wave velocity. The P-wave velocity was measured for the days 28 and 85 after mixing specimens, \ref{fig:07} and \ref{fig:08}) for the combinations $L_WL_B$ (Fig. \ref{fig:05}), $L_WEL_B$ (Fig. \ref{fig:06}), $H_WL_B$ (Fig. \ref{fig:07}), $H_WEL_B$ (Fig. \ref{fig:08}).

The compaction characteristics of the S/S soil mixed with binders in various proportions was evaluated upon the performed tests on the UCS. \section{Results}

\subsection{Phase 1: P-wave velocity and compressive strength}

Phase 1 of the experiment included testing of the two levels of binder quantity per $m^3$ of the dredged material: 150 kg and 120 kg. The results from the P-wave measurements are reported in Figure \ref{fig:02} and Figure \ref{fig:03}. The measurements show small mutual differences within the each recipe but a significant difference between the different recipes. For example, for samples in the $L_WH_B$ series, the Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS) varied between the 246 and 2262 kPa. In the $H_WL_B$ series, the UCS varied between the 96 and 1090 kPa, see Figure \ref{fig:04}. The results show high confirmation of the tests performed during the experiment sets even when determining the UCS. The results of the experiments are reported and summarised in Table \ref{tab:1}. The variation of velocity with curing time for all the S/S soil mixtures is summarised in Figure \ref{fig:03}.

\section{Discussion}
This study was performed to assess the feasibility of applying ultrasonic P-wave testing to evaluate stabilized soils with various combination of binders in different proportions of cement, slag and water. During curing progress, the reactions occurred between the soil samples and the stabilising binders in various proportions that have been tested and evaluated in this work. We observed that these reactions resulted in the increased stiffness of the soil samples. 

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{img/Fig_10} 
\end{center}
\caption{Evaluation of the factor experiment which shows linear effects between water ratio and the amount of binder. Increased water ratio results in a lower compressive strength despite constant amount of binder maintained in a soil mixture.}
\label{fig:10}
\end{figure}

It is explained by the increase of P-wave velocity during propagation of waves along with the increased stiffness of the soils which shows a direct correlation. Moreover, it was observed that the P-wave velocities of soil samples with binder ratio $L_WL_B$ and $H_WL_B$ (Figures \ref{fig:05} and \ref{fig:07}) were higher compared to the velocities of the mixtures with combinations $L_WEL_B$ and $H_WEL_B$ (Figures \ref{fig:06} and \ref{fig:08}). The P-wave velocities of the soils stabilized in ratio of binder $L_WL_B$ were the highest than the velocities obtained for other mixtures (Figure  \ref{fig:09}, red triangles). 

The properties of soils were also measured by the compressive strength testing in order to evaluate the suitability of the ground area for future construction in the Östrand's industrial area in Timrå municipality, Sweden. Ultrasonic P-wave velocity was measured in the SGI laboratory using collected soil samples stabilized by cement and slag in various proportions. Correlation of ultrasonic P-waves velocity with various type of stabilising binders (cement and slag), their ratio and curing time was investigated. Variations in the P-wave velocity with compression strength of the stabilized sediment samples was also analysed and evaluated as a series of statistical plots.

\section{Conclusions}

Various methods of estimating soil parameters for civil engineering have been used successfully for many years and reported in various studies \cite{Wuetal2021,Grabiec,Lemenkov2021c,Bahar}. However, existing theoretical guidelines and local specifications for the optimum design mixtures in specific soils and solidification process should always be supported by practical assessment of soil stability. This considers regional specifics and properties of local soils in northern Sweden, in order to ensure safety of constructions in a given area. It especially concerns the coastal areas with soft clayey, not stable soils, such as coastal areas of the Bothnian Bay of the Baltic Sea that should be stabilised and solidified before the construction works. To address this problem, a comprehensive series of laboratory tests has been performed in the SGI, to evaluate and establish the optimum binder-water ratio for different soil samples collected from the Östrand's industrial area in Timrå municipality, Sweden.
