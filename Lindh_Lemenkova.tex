\abtitle{Seismic velocity of P-waves to evaluate soil strength}
\title{Seismic velocity of P-wave propagation to evaluate soil strength for Svenska Cellulosa Aktiebolaget Biorefinery \"Ostrand AB, Timr\aa}

\abauthor{Author-1, Author-2}
\author{Author-1$^{1}$$^{2}$\email{email@blind.review} \\ Author-2$^{3}$\email{email@blind.review}}

\Address{$^1$ Affiliation-1 \\ $^2$ Affiliation-1 \\ $^3$ Affiliation-1}
  
\Abstract{\hl{Evaluating soil strength by geophysical methods using P-waves was undertaken in this study} to assess \hl{the effects of changed binder ratios on stabilisation and compression characteristics. The materials included} \hl{dredged sediments} collected in the seabed of Timr\aa \@ region, north Sweden. The \hl{Portland cement} (Basement CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1) and Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag \hl{(GGBFS) were used as stabilisers}. \hl{The} experiments were performed on behalf of the \hl{Svenska Cellulosa Aktiebolaget (SCA)} Biorefinery Östrand AB \hl{pulp mill}. \hl{Quantity of binder included 150, 120 and 100 kg}. The properties of soil were evaluated after 28, 42, 43, 70, 71 and 85 days of curing using applied geophysical methods of measuring the travel time of primary wave propagation. The P-waves were determined to evaluate the strength of stabilised soils. The results demonstrated variation of P-waves velocity depending on stabilising agent and curing time in various ratios: Low water / High binder ($L_WH_B$), High water / Low binder ($H_WL_B$) and percentage of agents (CEM II / A-V/GGBFS) as 30\%/70\%, 50\%/50\% and 70\%/30\%. The compression characteristics of soils were assessed using Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS). The P-wave velocities were higher for samples stabilized with $L_WH_B$ compared to those \hl{with} $H_WL_B$. The primary wave propagation increased over curing time for all stabilized mixes along with the increased UCS, which proves \hl{a tight} correlation with the increased strength of soil solidified by the agents. Increased water ratio gives a lower strength \hl{by maintained} amount of binder and vice versa.}

\Keywords{Unconfined Compressive Strength; P-wave velocity; stabilized soil; cement; slag}

\section{Introduction}

\hl{Bearing capacity of soil} plays a \hl{key} role \hl{in construction industry.} High strength of soil \hl{ensures} ground stability\hl{,} safety and sustainability of structures \cite{Czarnecki}. \hl{Increasing soil strength can be achieved by stabilising the soil with binders}. \hl{Selecting} optimal ratio of binders (e.g., cement, slag) is \hl{important} for increase of soil strength \cite{Lindh2001,Xizhong,LindhWinter}, \hl{but requires a practical evaluation of cement-slag-water ratio} for stabilising soil, as a series of tests. \hl{The strength of the stabilised soil} can be assessed by measuring \hl{velocities of P-wave propagation through} soil assessed after a curing period.

Various \hl{methods of evaluating soil properties, including} computer-based modelling, have been employed \hl{in existing studies.} These include estimating the \hl{porosity} \cite{Lemenkov2021a,Uyanik}\hl{,} density \cite{Consoli,Lemenkov2021b}, comparison of binder combinations \cite{Bernal}, \hl{admixtures} as cement replacement \cite{Eyo}, economic \hl{and environmental} assessment of S/S \cite{Rocha}, \hl{suction behaviour of soil to assess bearing capacity} \cite{Emeryetal}. \hl{The} S/S \hl{techniques} improve the silty clay structure by increasing cementation bonding and reducing the pore space \hl{in soil microstructure} \cite{Horpibulsuk}. Selecting 'hl{optimal} binder components (cement, cement kiln dust and slag) \hl{affects the results of} S/S of the sediments \cite{Lindh2021a} . 

Stabilisation of soil by the cementitious binder increases its strength, \hl{as} large pores in soil \hl{become} filled with binder \cite{Lindh202102}. As a result, total pore volume in a soil mass decreases, which makes it suitable for the \hl{industrial constructions}: roads or buildings. Experimental changing of ratio of binders \hl{(lime, cement, and lime-cement)} \hl{improves} soil \hl{strength and resistance}. For example, adding lime to the soil mass before \hl{S/S by} cement improves its physical and mechanical properties \cite{Mahmood}. \hl{The} S/S \hl{can apply various proportions of Portland cement} for treating soil contaminated with phenol choosing and carbon \cite{Suetal2009}. \hl{Thus}, soil could be effectively treated by the cement/activated-carbon S/S. \hl{Evaluation} of the uniaxial compressive strength \hl{(UCS)}, \cite{Diamantis} \hl{enables to highlight correlations} among the physical, dynamic and mechanical properties of the \hl{materials}. 

Soil \hl{stabilization} is commonly used in civil engineering. \hl{Evaluation of soil} is generally based on the \hl{empirical} assessment of \hl{its geotechnical parameters} \cite{BrunarskiDohojda,CzarneckiGemert,Kallenetal2016}. It forms the fundamental basis for \hl{estimating} the foundation capacity of soil \hl{and assessment of} its suitability for building structures. \hl{Soil} may be processed from the \hl{\emph{in-situ}} collected samples using laboratory\hl{-based} tests \hl{to measure responses to stress}. \hl{Recent approaches tend to apply} the non-destructive approaches \hl{in} soil testing. \hl{Ultrasonic testing by P-wave velocities is a rapidly growing application in evaluation of the stabilized soils}. \hl{The} geophysical approaches in civil engineering, such as measuring P-waves\hl{,} become \hl{effective} alternative to the traditional methods\hl{, as more robust and non-destructive methods}. \hl{Many} studies \hl{applied} geophysical methods in geotechnical measurements \hl{to} improve the evaluation of the soil properties by measuring P-waves propagation velocity in samples \cite{Frattaetal2005,Zhaoetal2022,Zhangetal2021,Guetal2021,Weietal2022}.

\hl{Estimating the} wave velocity for assessing soil mechanical property can be advantageously used in \hl{the} field and laboratory under real and controlled conditions \cite{Hussien}. For instance, measuring shear wave velocity is a \hl{fast} means for the determination of small-strain shear modulus in \hl{materials} \cite{Leong}. \hl{Estimating} wave velocity \hl{is based on measuring} speed and amplitude \hl{of} sound waves \hl{that} pass through soil. \hl{Detecting signals of waves provides a basis for analysis} of \hl{soil} conductivity and petrophysical properties\hl{:} bulk density, porosity, clay content, strength. \hl{This is} because the velocity of seismic waves depends on the material properties and generally increases with higher density \hl{and rigidity of material} \cite{ElSayed}. However, since stiffness increases at faster rate compared to density, seismic velocity is faster in \hl{a} denser soil \cite{Shietal2010}. \hl{Moreover, the} velocity strongly depends on \hl{the} porosity of the material and significantly increases even with a small decrease in porosity \cite{Foti}. 

\hl{Measuring} shear wave velocity for estimation of stiffness and strength of the soft and silty \hl{clayey} soil \hl{is a reported practice} \cite{Cheshomi,Wuetal2021}. \hl{Void} ratio in soil and effective mean normal stress can be estimated for analysis of the large-strain response \hl{and relationship between soil strength and wave propagation} \cite{Liuetal2020}. \hl{Strength of soil can also be assessed through} the analysis of cohesive behaviour \cite{Gecys}. \hl{More examples of the evaluation of} the compressive strength include the analysis of biomodification of the recycled concrete aggregate \cite{Grabiec2019}, \hl{or optimised methods for fabrication of composites through modified mechanical and tribological properties for increasing their compressive strength and hardness} \cite{Manikandan} . 

In this paper, the \hl{stabilization and solidification (S/S)} of soils were performed as a part of the reinforcement work \hl{in the selected coastal area of the Baltic Sea, northern Sweden}. The \hl{Svenska Cellulosa Aktiebolaget (SCA)} Biorefinery Östrand AB plans to build a new biorefinery for the production of fuel in form of petrol and diesel \hl{using} biological raw materials. This construction is planned to extend the existing infrastructure of the pulp mill in \hl{the} industrial area located in Timr\aa \@ municipality \hl{Fig.} \ref{fig:01}. \hl{Planned works require} extra area \hl{to} be \hl{prepared through soil stabilisation and more space gained for the construction area}.

The aim of this work was to \hl{stabilise} the \hl{sediments dredged from} the seabed \hl{using binders} to increase the compressive strength of soil. The properties of soil were evaluated using applied geophysical methods of measuring velocities of the \hl{propagating} P-wave that pass through the samples.

\section{Methods}
The purpose of this study is to investigate the properties of soil after S/S \hl{treatment}, to define the best combination of binders \hl{ that can improve its geotechnical} properties. The workflow consisted of the \hl{by measuring} velocity of ultrasonic primary waves \hl{using geophysical methods}  \cite{Lindh2018,Rydenetal}. 

\hl{Measuring P-wave velocity is a} fast, robust and straightforward technique to analysing physical and mechanical properties \hl{of} soils. \hl{Its non-destructive nature enables the same specimen to be measured unlimited number of times without being damaged. The specimens were measured using the Free-Free Resonant (FFR) testing, aimed to measure natural frequencies of free vibration of the tested soil specimens.}

\hl{The P-wave velocity was measured in a longitudinal direction. Practically, using the values of frequencies received from the FFR tests one can estimate the compression strength of soil using correlations between the wave velocity and the undrained shear strength} \cite{Ahnberg}. \hl{Besides, geotechnical soil parameters can be determined using FFR, such as shear wave, seismic moduli, stiffness moduli and Poisson's ratio} \cite{VERASTEGUIFLORES2015943}.

\subsection{Materials}
\hl{The} tests were conducted \hl{in a geotechnical laboratory of the Swedish Geotechnical Institute (SGI) using sediment samples originally sampled by SCA} stabilised by a combination of cement (Basement CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1) and Ground Granulated Blast Furnace Slag (GGBFS), \hl{trade name Bremen slag SS EN 15167-1}. The \hl{UCS of the stabilised soil was measured by the} P-wave \hl{travel time through} soil \hl{samples}. \hl{The major geotechnical parameters of soil include shear strength, permeability, compaction and particle size. In this study we obtained shear strength parameters changed from 48 to 1093 kPa for 3 ratios of stabilisers (Table} \ref{tab:1}) \hl{The soil compaction shown maximal dry density at $1,12 g/cm^3$ by 38.75\% of water in a binder. The clay content in the tested soil was 7.6\%.}

\hl{Mixtures were prepared in the SGI by mixing soil samples with specific types of stabilisers (CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1 and GGBFS). The amounts and ratios of stabilisers were varied between batches which were treated with a combination of stabilisers in the following ratios: 30/70\%, 50/50\% and 70/30\%}. The \hl{workflow included} three levels of binder quantity in kg per $m^3$ of the dredged material. \hl{Various} levels of binder quantity per $m^3$ of the dredged material \hl{were tested}: 150 kg\hl{,} 120 kg \hl{and} 100 kg. \hl{The} quality requirements included \hl{the} following conditions: \hl{UCS} of at least 140 kPa (ca $\geq$ 70 kPa) after 90 days; replacements in the stabilized material \textless 5 cm two years after the S/S. The quality objective regarding \hl{soil} permeability constituted geotechnical \hl{quality} requirements. 

\hl{The binders CEM II / A-V, SS EN 197-1 and GGBFS} were selected as the stabilising agents \hl{due to their} quality and availability. Both \hl{binders} are available on the commercial market and have been used in previous projects of \hl{the SGI}, including the Arendal\hl{,} port of Gothenburg \cite{Lindh2021a}. The GGBS is one of the by-products of iron and steel, \hl{and economic as a} binder for large quantities of soil \cite{Yuksel} \hl{and} beneficial to \hl{the} environment \hl{for as a replacement of Portland cement}. \hl{The} GGBS added to soil as an admixture significantly enhances its mechanical strength during the S/S \cite{Wangetal2020}. The Basecement Slite produced by the Cementa (Heidelberg Cement Group) (https://www.cementa.se/en/bascement-eng) replaces the previous Byggcementen CEM II / A-L, \hl{due} to better technical characteristics. \subsection{Techniques}
\hl{Joint analysis of the UCS and seismic velocity data was used to detect the strength of soil specimens. The UCS aimed to determine the strength of a cohesive clayey soil by measuring the maximum axial compressive stress that a cohesive soil specimen can withstand under zero confining stress.}

\hl{The sediments were dredged using a $1\space m^3$ bucket, filled into the separate containers and sent to the SGI for testing. The soil in each container was homogenised: sediments were placed in a mixer and mixed with added binders and water. Following ratios were used for stabilisation}: Low Water / High Binder ($L_WH_B$), High Water / Low Binder ($H_WL_B$), Low Water / Extra Low Binder ($L_WEL_B$). The ratio of binder varied as cement /slag as 50\/50\% and cement/slag proportion as 30\/70\% and 70\/30\%. The \hl{material} was stored in climate rooms at a constant moisture and T 7$^{\circ}$C). The aggregate sample B6b was divided into the two barrels with tight-fitting lids. \hl{Afterwards}, one of the barrels was mixed with more \hl{added} water to simulate a higher water ratio of the dredged material.  

The \hl {workflow followed the technical} specifications of the Swedish Institute for Standards \hl{(SIS)} \cite{SIS2018b}. The tests were performed on the day of sample processing and subsequent \hl{on} days 28, 43 and 70 of curing for the case of $H_WL_B$ \hl{mix}; days 28, 42 and 71 for $L_WH_B$; 28 and 85 for $L_WEL_B$, respectively, Fig. \ref{fig:02}. \hl{Mixing and thinning process lasted about 5 min by means of mechanical stirring to achieve a homogeneous structure. The stabilized sediments were then filled into the plastic barrels. For each mix of stabilized soil, the specimens were cast into the 15 pistons with sampling sleeves and with covers.}

When the sample is on a foam rubber pad, it can vibrate freely. These vibrations were measured using the accelerometer attached to the samples, adapted to the size and mass of the specimens (in our case, the cylindrical form of specimens)}.

The P-wave velocity has been measured on three cases for all collected samples in connection with tests on the \hl{Uniaxial Compressive Strength (UCS)} for relevant soil samples. The workflow applied the existing methodology of seismic velocity testing in geotechnical works, originally elaborated by SIS \cite{SIS2018a,SIS2019}. The experiments with $L_WH_B$ proportions of binder were performed \hl{following} the compression on the 28, 42 and 71 days after mixing soil \hl{samples} with binder (Fig. \ref{fig:02}). 

Likewise, the experiments with $H_WL_B$ \hl{ratios} of binder were performed on samples on days 28, 43 and 70 after mixing sediments with binder (Fig. \ref{fig:02}). The P-wave velocity was measured on the three days of curing \hl{for} the both cases of ratio ($H_WL_B$ and $L_WH_B$). The measurements were recorded, statistically processed as graphs and visualised in the plots, presented in the Results section.

The comparative analysis of the compressive strength for samples with different \hl{mixes} ($L_WH_B$ versus $H_WL_B$) and proportions of cement/slag as 70/30, 50/50 and 30/70 were recorded, \hl{evaluated} and visualised in Fig. \ref{fig:04} \hl{where the} regression lines \hl{is shown} for the two cases: ratio $L_WH_B$ (green line) and ratio $H_WL_B$ (magenta line). The compaction \hl{of} soil \hl{stabilised by} binders in various \hl{ratios} was evaluated upon the performed tests on the UCS. \hl{The mixes} were prepared at various content of water/binder (cement and slag) which is representative for \hl{the} evaluation of each case for \hl{the} stabilized \hl{samples}. The tests were conducted using \hl{the} available equipment of SGI according to existing workflow of \hl{SIS} for geotechnical works \cite{SIS2017}. The requirement for testing UCS was set as 140 kPa with a safety factor of 2, \emph{i.e.}, 280 kPa, (Fig. \ref{fig:04}, \hl{red dotted reference line}). \hl{The testing method of shear strength was based on the existing SIS standards and technical guidance} \cite{SIS1991}.

\hl{A} correlation between \hl{the} binder content and water content in \hl{a soil mix} was assessed and visualised (Fig. \ref{fig:05}) as a factor experiment \hl{showing} linear effect between \hl{water} ratio and the amount of binder in the processed soil. \hl{Thus, it} shows that the increased water ratio results in a lower compressive strength of soil, while the amount of binder is kept maintained. The \hl{seismic} tests included \hl{the} determination of \hl{the} velocity of P-waves in soil \hl{stabilized by} binders in various ratios: $L_WL_B$, $L_WEL_B$, $H_WL_B$ and $H_WEL_B$ (Fig. \ref{fig:05}).

\section{Discussion}

\hl{The assessment of the material properties is one of most important problem in geotechnical studies and construction industry. One of the main challenges is find a correct method for the optimum design mixtures of binders used for effective S/S of soils}. Various methods of estimating soil parameters were used successfully in civil engineering \cite{Wuetal2021,Grabiec,Lemenkov2021c,Bahar}. \hl{In seismic methods, including ultrasonic tests, the P-wave velocity increases along with the increased rigidity of medium, which is an expected phenomenon in cementation process.} \hl{The correlations for different mixtures and the proportions of binders may vary for different case studies. Therefore, besides the existing theoretical guidelines such experiments should always be supported by the practical assessment of soil strength and stability} 

\hl{This study demonstrated the results of variations of the P-wave velocity and compression characteristics of soil stabilized by various binder ratios over time in Timr\aa \@ locality, Sweden. The novelty of the paper consists in the documented application of geophysical methods to study strength of soil samples collected at the sites of planned industrial construction on behalf of the SCA Biorefinery \"Ostrand AB. The advanced approach was presented to evaluate the UCS in soil stabilised with various binder ratios using P-wave velocity. \section{Conclusions and Recommendations}

This study \hl{assessed} the feasibility of \hl{geophysical methods of} ultrasonic P-wave testing \hl{to} evaluate \hl{soil strength}. \hl{Because soil was stabilized} with various binder \hl{ratios, we evaluated the effects of} different \hl{amounts} of cement, slag and water \hl{on stabilisation}. \hl{The UCSs of stabilised soil treated with three combinations of stabilisers (CEM II-GGBS in ratios 30/70\%, 50/50\% and 70/30\%) are shown in Table 1. As can be seen, soil treated with ratio 30/70\% of CEM II-GGBS has higher strength which is caused by the high amounts of Ca responsible for the higher strength of stabilisers}. 

\hl{Along with} curing progress, the reactions occurred between the soil samples and the stabilising \hl{agents} in various proportions that \hl{were} tested and evaluated\hl{.} We \hl{noted} that these reactions resulted in the increased stiffness \hl{and rigidity} of \hl{soil}. It is explained by the increase of \hl{the} P-wave velocity of \hl{wave propagation} along with the increased stiffness of \hl{soil} which shows a \hl{tight} correlation. \hl{The P-wave velocity depends not only from the dynamic modulus of elasticity (stiffness) and Poisson ratio, but mainly on apparent density. This is one of the key parameters that should be evaluated for all tested soil-cement materials. Apparent density is the essential material property that correlates with the speed of elastic wave propagation in the material. This property gives more precise information on soil porosity reduction due to the addition of mineral binder.}

Moreover, it was observed that the P-wave velocities of soil \hl{stabilised} with $L_WL_B$ and $H_WL_B$ were higher compared to \hl{those of} $L_WEL_B$ and $H_WEL_B$ (Figure \ref{fig:05}). The P-wave velocities of \hl{soil} stabilized \hl{with binders of ratio} $L_WL_B$ were the highest \hl{compared to those} obtained for other mixtures (Figure  \ref{fig:04}, red triangles). \hl{The GGBFS was used along with CEM II / A-V, because the low calcium content of slag improved stabilising of clayey soil. Admixtures of GGBFS contributed to increase the compressive strength of soil-cement mixes}. 

\hl{For future studies, a series of tests can be applied to model the elastic behaviour of soil, determine small-strain shear modulus and the response to the dynamic loading. Since tested mixtures are incorporated into the subsoil, a complement to the given analysis would include testing responses of specimens to the triaxial stresses using triaxial apparatus supported by bender elements. An example is the bender elements test to measure the propagation of the S and P waves on mixtures built into the subsoil. A comparison of strength by uniaxial and triaxial tests would be beneficial practically for engineering reasons and to assess the applicability of the simplified method.}
